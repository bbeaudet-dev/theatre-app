Once the shutdown reached professional sports, there was an [episode of LastWeekTonight](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SnZLBvePL0o) that featured a YouTube channel called [Jelle's Marble Runs](https://www.youtube.com/@JellesMarbleRuns), which hosts an annual Olympics-style competition - for marbles! Fascinated, inspired, obsessed, I began religiously followly the MarbleLympics. The joy of building imaginary worlds came surging back, and I began to dream of building something like Jelle's Marble Runs - something with endless creativity, enabling others to feel that same joy of world-building, and even to make a living off of, if I was lucky.

Being a lifelong Smash Bros fan, a student with spare time, and a kid with a dream to make a living building imaginary worlds, these marble-themed Olympic games, along with some [Olympic-themed Smash Bros videos by Little Z](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mppbxovBoY8&t=393s), inspired me to create a new world, channeling my younger self / days of stuffed animal basketball drafts, and drafting the entire Smash Bros roster into 24 different teams that would compete in a variety of creative events, often using custom sports-themed stages.

With the job market in shambles, I started a postgrad degree at JCU in Cleveland, OH and got a part-time job as a line cook. 
Smash Bros Olympics was my spare time project, in between working and school, and of course my break from the depression of COVID

Having enjoyed video editing previously, I made a [fun trailer for the "Smash Bros Olympics"](https://youtu.be/YUAcVOsvBD0?si=QDAU8YAFZ4mbS8Sw) and posted it on a new YouTube channel under the same name. "12 events, 24 teams, only 1 will emerge victorious!"

When the trailer got more attention than I expected, I started working on trailers / hype videos for each of the teams. Looking back, I was much more interested in designing the "rules" and "ideas" of this world than I was actually running and publishing the annual events. Coupled with a naivety about how much work would be involved in putting on the Smash Bros Olympics, and YouTube videos in general, meant that 2 months and 27 videos later, my YouTube channel was essentially still _just trailers_.

As time went on, I found myself pulled in different directions. I was still enjoying Smash Bros and the project I had created, and I had a great setup for recording new content - however, my video editing process was unbearably slow and I was suscepitble to endless perfectionism and refinement, and the transition from school to a full time job prompted the same deluge of "rational thoughts" as when I first started college: "it was time to get **serious** - get a job, make a living for myself, support a family. There was no time for silly YouTube channels or imaginary world-building.

As I've talked about in another post, there's always been some element of this "play", this inner child, in my life. From stuffed animals to LEGOs to Minecraft, and then, to mechanical engineering. Designing real objects that do real things for real people in the real world, and getting paid real money for it!

And then I fell in love with coding. With peering behind the curtain and fiddling with the actual code, the actual instructions of actual things. And of course games and game dev, and maybe now how I might make a life for myself.

And now, Benana Split.

We don’t even really experience life as a series of linear, logical, evenly spaced steps, like maybe we think of time, or maybe like how you’re reading this post; it’s more like digging a tunnel and popping up every so often to see the paths ahead and behind, feeling the warmth again.

But I didn't experience life like you're reading it. It's really only now that I'm connecting this thread from my childhood, through every phase of my life, to my current situation. Life is like digging a tunnel through the earth, trying to find your way, and once in a while popping up above the surface, letting the light flood the path behind you and the potential roads ahead, like a little prairie dog in those clear bubble domes in the zoo, and we see the dots connecting, where paths converged and diverged, maybe led us in circles, and when you blazed forward with a mission in mind and a purpose of fire. I remember popping up as a kid, vividly, being proud of my spreadsheets, being a kid that got to do exactly what he wanted, just living life, unaware of growing up. I remember not fitting in at school, being a selective mute, being the special one, the lonely one, the odd one out. I remember finally breaking free from my muteness, finally joining the world, becoming like everyone else, overcoming fear. I remember starting middle school, the bullying, being the one desperately seeking attention, approval, friends. I remember falling down a similar path during those times, backtracking, retracing old tunnels and starting to dig them up again, destroying the evidence, ashamed of my little spreadsheets, afraid that showing them to anyone, letting them ever see the light of day again, would permanently scar my reputation and bar me from ever being "cool". I probably deleted some, some that I wasn't able to recover like the ones you're seeing today. The cool kids were interested in girls, in being funny and popular, in talking about cool things like parties and money and drugs and alcohol and sex. My joy of building worlds turned into shame, into a secret that I shielded from everyone, even the people that knew me then, like family and old friends. I remember carrying that same shame into high school, where I fit in even less because I always got along well with girls anyways. I remember a breaking point, a reckoning, and a rebirth, a newfound confidence, acceptance. I remember retracing my steps again as I entered college, desperately trying to find out who I was, seeking identity, trying to reinvent myself. I remember popping up in the creek behind Marcum, dying and being born anew, a blank slate. I remember the sense of opportunity of moving to a new city, to be on my own. I remember descending into the depths, of shame, hiding myself, lying. Even while I was making this channel. I remember putting in a lot of effort to change my path, to dig a new tunnel. I remember carrying that hope into Easthaven. I remember descending into indignancy there, feeling powerless and broken and shattered. I remember the grieving process, the re-grieving, of ADHD acceptance. I remember having a sense of power, of freedom, of potential. I remember rediscovering the files, being mature enough to see it as a foundational part of my childhood, and feeling overjoyed being able to share them with family and friends, experiencing anew the joy, the absurdity, the creativity. And then the next time I popped up, I was shattered again, hating my job and craving something new. I remember feeling hopeless, impossible to keep up with others' expectations of me. I remember going to New York, scared, convinced I was not good enough, I was broken. I remember working so hard that I felt even more broken, entirely, at the core, not good enough, why am I here? I remember being reborn, finding a life that just might make me happy, a routine that might just make me healthy, people that I might just like being around, a city that might just make me feel alive. I remember feeling robbed of that, like I was forcibly taken away from that life that I had just discovered, the place I had just discovered myself. As of late, I've been feeling broken again. Lost, hopeless, out of energy. Angry. But now, I can at least see the thread. I have at least popped up my head again and seen the light illuminate my past, I feel the warmth on my face, the aliveness of life. I see my possible futures, and I see the thread that runs from these futures all the way back to my past, as far as Ben goes.

Maybe one of the threads is that I like to invent my own rules, my own systems, to push things beyond their limits, to build worlds that I can live in, worlds that might just be more fit for me than the one I live in. Or maybe it's opposition, stubbornness. They are 2 sides of the same coin, but in life, we have the ability to choose which side we see. Maybe I just want to build things with my hands, or create new things for the sake of novelty and not getting bored. Maybe I build them to escape commitment, or because I can't accept responsibility for making mistakes, for ruining the world I'm in. Maybe I escape to new worlds so I'm not a burden on anyone there.
More literally, maybe I've just always loved playing games incorrectly! Making up my own rulesets! Building a world that I can call my own.

So, Benana Split, for now. Popping up, back down into the tunnels.

While some may say it's childish, silly, that I'm not contributing anything to the world or to society, that it's just make believe, I say that we tell stories so that we may live through them, learn from them, pop up to the surface more often and feel the warmth of being alive, see the people all around you living life in the same world, but completely different realities. The human spirit is creative, it's free, it wants to fly, not be bogged down with nonfiction. If everyone's reality is different, how can there be anything that fits into a unary "nonfiction"?
They may be imaginary, but they're the realest thing I've found, or the closest thing I've found to feeling real, or the thing that makes me feel the closest to being real.
It gets at the questions of what is valuable at the end of the day? Money? zoom out. Value? zoom out. Functioning and thriving society? zoom out. A thriving species? zoom out. We're on a rock. Zoom in. We're all just on a rock, temporarily. We all need to eat, breathe, poop, and be warmed and cooled and a place to be. What if we could make it even better? A sense of belonging, freedom of expression, opportunity. Enlightenment, fulfillment, achievement and potential. Ok then, what's really important? Connection, helping each other. We're all just in this together. Let's play a game together and laugh at the absurdity of existence together. Let's have a coffee. Let's enjoy a sunrise, a sunset, a bonfire, live music, trees blowing in the wind, flying in an airplane among the clouds, rain, snow, continental shifts, the universe. Let's try to feel right now how we felt when we were children. When the universe was endless, and we had endless curiosity. Forget age, taboo, "supposed to"s and shoulds, forget maturity, acting your age, growing up, manning up, growing a pair. Forget careers.

Let's build worlds together.
